# One month in the Fabric Dojo 🥋

In April 2025, I joined the **Fabric Dojo**, a private community led by Will Needham. My goal: move from the open-source data engineering tools I'd used for years to **Microsoft Fabric**.

Fabric was new to me. Data engineering wasn't. So the challenge was learning the new interface and features, not the concepts. I finished all the classes in one month.

## The belt log

- **Dataflow Gen2 and data pipelines → yellow belt (level 3).** How to pull data from many sources, load it into a lakehouse or warehouse, and transform it. I earned the yellow belt in a few days.
- **Data warehouse and T-SQL → level 4.** 28 exercises, from T-SQL basics and OLAP concepts to Kimball dimensional modeling, surrogate keys and slowly changing dimensions. I liked how Will built the learning curve, from simple exercises to a real project.
- **CI/CD.** Version control and deployment in Fabric, item by item, with Azure DevOps, VS Code, Power BI project files (PBIP) and SQL database DACPAC files. In my past on-premise jobs, I was often a "solo data warrior" who had to invent my own workarounds for this. Luckily, my friends Yussin Hidayat and Aslah Fuadin had already taught me Git on a real project.
- **Lakehouse, notebooks and PySpark → blue belt (level 5).** From the basics to an advanced data cleaning challenge, plus orchestration with notebooks, shortcuts and mirroring. My Python and Jupyter experience helped a lot.
- **Real-time and streaming.** Spark Structured Streaming, Eventstreams, Eventhouse, KQL and real-time dashboards. This one made me think about my 18 years in telecom network monitoring. Imagine streaming network data live, detecting problems immediately, and showing them on a real-time dashboard!
- **Quality assurance. My favorite!** Data quality checks and data auditing. I realized this is my strongest skill, built over many years: checking data availability, the integration between operational systems and the data warehouse, and the final reports, to make sure everything is consistent and accurate.

By the end, I was **#1 on the Fabric Dojo leaderboard for April 2025.**

## After the Dojo

In August 2025, I passed the **DP-700: Microsoft Fabric Data Engineer Associate** exam.

I wanted this certification for a special reason. Unlike Power BI, it's hard to show data engineering skills in public. There's no beautiful interface, just flows and scripts. A certification helps show the work nobody sees.

I also kept exploring. In July 2025, I followed Ansh Lamba's six-hour end-to-end **Azure Databricks** project: a data lake, Unity Catalog, Delta tables, PySpark, slowly changing dimensions and streaming. My first impression: Databricks has everything you need for a data warehouse. I haven't seen how it performs with huge data yet, or what it costs.

## What I learned

- **Find your community.** One month with a good teacher and an active community taught me more than a year alone.
- **Old skills transfer.** Twenty years of on-premise data work made the cloud tools much easier to learn.

Thank you, Will Needham, and the Fabric Dojo community. Thanks also to Aleksi Partanen for his YouTube videos, and to Pak Feris Thia, my mentor on my first data warehouse back in 2009.
