# My first year in healthcare

## A new adventure 🎉

In October 2025, I started a new adventure as a **Quality & Data Analyst at Johns Hopkins Aramco Healthcare (JHAH).**

I wouldn't have been ready without some truly inspiring mentors:
- **Injae Park** and the Power BI Park community. He did much more than teach Power BI. He helped me build my personal brand, showed me how to create a professional portfolio, and always motivated me to keep learning. To me, he's a professor, a mentor and a motivator. 감사합니다!
- **Gustaw Dudek**, for adding the corporate layer to my data visualization skills. Dziękuję!
- **Naif Al Malki**, my data analytics mentor for many years. جزاك الله خيرا

## Week one: 60% people

I always believed that about 40% of a data analyst's job is working with people: understanding what the business needs, learning the work process, and talking to stakeholders.

After one week in healthcare, I found it's even more. In my team, it's closer to **60%.** I spend more time talking to people than working with data.

That made a productivity system a must. When you work with people, many requests come and go quickly, often verbally. It's easy to forget something important.

> "Our brains are for having ideas, not holding them." (David Allen)

So I started using Jeff Su's **C.O.R.E.** system:
- **Capture:** write everything down immediately.
- **Organize:** put each note in the right place, with a due date or a tag.
- **Review:** every day, turn raw notes into calendar blocks and tasks.
- **Engage:** do the work you planned.

## Month two: the chief's reaction 🎯

My leader and I presented an analysis to one of our company's chiefs. He was very impressed with the visualization, especially because it **delivered the message clearly, with very little explanation from us.** That's the power of good visuals, using PowerPoint and Power BI together.

I used several advanced front-end techniques from The Data Vis Forge, a bit of SVG "spice" inspired by Injae Park, and text graphics inspired by Armand van Amersfoort's web-style reports. Thank you to my manager and the team for giving me room to experiment, and the chance to present to leadership.

## A new data friend 🤝

Around the same time, I had lunch at an Egyptian restaurant in Al Khobar with a new friend: a senior data analyst consultant from India who has worked here for many years.

We talked about data management: metadata, data quality, data models, and matching our work to the business glossary. He first said he was a fan of my LinkedIn posts. By the end of lunch, I was definitely a fan of his!

He asked how I learned data visualization. I told him the truth: I never formally studied UI/UX. It's just something I love, and I've used it in my presentations since I was a kid. I'm still learning it from others.

## Finding mentors 🥋

When I was preparing for this role, I watched many videos from Joshua Matlock's "Data Wizardry" YouTube channel. One piece of advice stuck with me: **seek out a mentor as soon as possible.** It doesn't matter how experienced you are. Healthcare and analytics are both always changing.

I followed his advice, and I got lucky. My colleagues are senior people with clinical backgrounds who know the healthcare business inside out. My manager has a strong background in data management. I was already surrounded by great mentors.

Josh also said to get to know data people in other departments. So I joined a healthcare data management workshop run by colleagues from another division. It was designed for doctors, nurses and pharmacists, but I learned far more than I expected. Thank you, Dr. Shareef AlMubarak and Dr. Eman Sherif. We also invited an expert from our IT architecture team, who explained how the data stores behind our analytics fit together.

Thank you to my director, Jalal Al-Alwan, and to JHAH, for the continuous support.

## What I learned

- **Stay close to the business.** In healthcare, that means learning from clinical people every day.
- Starting in a new environment can be hard. Mentors, and connections with other teams, make all the difference.
