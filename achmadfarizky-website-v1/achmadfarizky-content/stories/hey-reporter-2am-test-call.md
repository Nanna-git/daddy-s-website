# Hey reporter, you're part of the brain!

In 2005, I started my career at Telkomsel as a radio network quality engineer. And honestly, I didn't feel like a "real" telecom engineer.

My colleagues travelled overseas for technical training. They went out to the field with drive test tools like TEMS, Nemo and QualiPoc, and they handled real network hardware. Me? I stayed at my desk, staring at spreadsheets.

## The 2 AM test call

I wanted to be one of them so badly. One night, I begged to go out with one of my colleagues. They sent me to a rural site at 2:00 AM, just to make a test call.

One test call. In the middle of the night. In the middle of nowhere.

I was so happy, like a kid in an amusement park.

## The desk guy

But the truth is, I loved working with data from the start. I never just delivered numbers. I kept simplifying our processes and looking for insights that could help my team work better. I learned UNIX scripting by myself to automate small things. I built a web dashboard and a live map of our network with Google Earth.

I was just jealous of the deep technical knowledge and the privileges my colleagues had.

Then one day, I realized something big: **I wasn't just their calculator guy. I was guiding their decisions.**

A business doesn't run on equipment alone. It runs on good data, and on people who take care of it. Today we call that being "data-driven."

## Twenty years later: the brain

Late in 2025, through the posts of Elijah Joshua Pryor, I discovered DAMA International and the CDMP certification. I started reading the DMBOK, the big book of data management, and I was amazed.

What I always thought was a small back-office job, the reporting, the data cleaning, the definitions, is actually part of the organization's central brain.

I studied the DMBOK deeply to make sure my work followed global best practices. I passed the fundamentals exam, then the specialist exams in data modeling, data warehousing and BI, and data quality. In August 2026, I officially earned the **CDMP Master** certification.

## Thank you

A big thank you to my early managers and team leaders who kept me at my desk with Excel: **Petrus Sila, Sorpa EA Togatorop, Ferinaldi Kombes and Akhmad Madces.** You saw where my work created real value before I did. You helped me build a career I love.

Thank you also to the people who shared their knowledge while I was studying: Nicole Janeway Bills (Data Strategy Professionals), Howard Diesel (Modelware Systems and DAMA South Africa), and Georgi Smarts (Udemy).

So, to every "reporter" out there sitting at a desk full of spreadsheets: do good work. You're part of the brain.

**Never underestimate where a simple spreadsheet can take you.**
