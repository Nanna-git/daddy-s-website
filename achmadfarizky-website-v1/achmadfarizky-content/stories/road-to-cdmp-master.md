# Road to CDMP Master 🥋

## "Where have I been for the last 15 years?"

In 2025, I started following Elijah Joshua Pryor on LinkedIn. His posts about data management concepts kept connecting with my own experience. Through him, I learned about an amazing book: the **Data Management Body of Knowledge (DMBOK)**, from DAMA International, a global non-profit dedicated to data management best practices.

The DMBOK covers the whole data world, in four logical steps:
- data modeling, storage, integration and security,
- data quality, architecture and metadata,
- data governance, master and reference data, data warehousing and BI, documents and content,
- big data and data science.

I bought the book and started reading. And I kept asking myself: **where have I been for the last 15 years?** The first edition came out in 2009, and the second in 2017. I just hadn't noticed it.

I wish I'd read it long ago. It would have made the data solutions I delivered even stronger, and it would have given me better arguments when asking customers to approve them.

My favorite lines from the book are simple: **data only brings value when it's used.** And managing data means managing the quality of data.

I was also happy to discover that Saudi Arabia already had an official DAMA chapter, DAMA Saudi, since 2023.

## Fundamentals: two weeks

I had become a bit obsessed with professional certifications, because they push me to build a solid foundation. After DP-700, PL-300 and CHDA, I started the **Certified Data Management Professional (CDMP)** path.

I studied for two weeks and passed the **Data Management Fundamentals** exam, with a score above 80%. That made me eligible for the Master path: pass two specialist exams with 80% or more, and you get the CDMP Master.

## Data Modeling and Design: 2 AM, 90%

For two months, I went deep into data modeling. I woke up at 2 AM to take the exam, and I passed with **90%, at Master level.**

It felt like all my past data experience had finally been put together into one clear framework. Three things helped me:
1. **Real experience.** To reach Master level, studying alone isn't enough. You need real projects behind you.
2. **Leading a real project now.** My director at JHAH, Jalal Al-Alwan, gave me the chance to lead our new data project, so I practiced modeling at work while studying.
3. **Good resources.** Nicole Janeway Bills (Data Strategy Professionals) and Veronica and Howard Diesel (Modelware Systems).

## Data Warehousing and BI: a belief made stronger

Many experts say the difference between a junior and an expert analyst isn't technical skill. It's where they spend their time. Juniors focus on loading data and making charts look nice. Experts first understand the business needs and build a strong data foundation.

I've always believed that data lineage, data dictionaries and metadata are **not extras. They're mandatory.** If you ignore data quality now and plan to "fix it later," you're building a house on sand. That's why I spend more time at the start of every project on these things, with or without manager support.

Studying the DW/BI knowledge area made this belief even stronger. I passed the **DW/BI specialist** exam, my third CDMP exam above 80%.

A special thank you to **Pak Akhmad Madces and Pak Reza Lesmana.** In 2009, when I knew nothing, you brought me into a giant real-world data warehouse project with Pentaho. Your design was amazing, and I still use those concepts today. Thanks also to **Pak Feris Thia** for the Pentaho Indonesia community and the support.

## Data Quality: garbage in, garbage out?

People often say bad data comes from wrong inputs during data entry. That's true. But studying for the **Data Quality specialist** exam, I learned it's **not the main cause.**

Data quality is a problem of **people, processes and business culture.** It's not just typing mistakes or missing software. Technology alone can't fix it. Bad processes cause more problems than typos. The real root cause is a lack of management and governance.

This exam surprised me. About half the questions were about dealing with executives and stakeholders, and how to start a data quality program, things you won't find written in the DMBOK. I've worked on data quality for a decade, but mostly on the technical side. This exam needed the mindset of a program manager. It was hard.

After waiting almost three weeks, I received the badge: **Data Quality Specialist.** Alhamdulillah.

## CDMP Master

In August 2026, it all came together: **CDMP Master.** (The full story of what that means to me is in [Hey reporter, you're part of the brain!](hey-reporter-2am-test-call.md).)

Along the way, DAMA Indonesia in Jakarta listed me among the Indonesian CDMP holders. Thank you, Pak Alex Budiyanto. To my fellow data people in Indonesia: let's keep promoting good data management in our own organizations.

## What I learned

- **Data management is the "core of the core."** Analytics is the part people see. Data management is what makes it trustworthy.
- Thank you, Elijah Joshua Pryor, for opening that door.
