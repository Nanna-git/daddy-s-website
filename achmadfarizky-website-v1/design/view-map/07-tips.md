````md
## Tips for people with a background like mine

For engineers from non-IT fields who want to move into data, for anyone mid-career who wants to learn something new, and for anyone starting over.
````

### V02 · T2 Text+Scene · 34 words

````md
1. **Learn the small skill nobody around you has.** A little programming made me useful in a telecom team full of hardware engineers. You don't need to be an expert, just a bit ahead.
````

### V03 · T2 Text+Scene · 24 words

````md
2. **Stay close to the business.** Step away from your desk. Ask people about their daily problems. Learn their language before you build anything.
````

### V04 · T2 Text+Scene · 28 words

````md
3. **Build with what you already have.** Expensive tools don't make you valuable. Solving a real problem with existing tools, in a way your colleagues can maintain, does.
````

### V05 · T2 Text+Scene · 22 words

````md
4. **Join a community, and just do the thing.** Follow the instructions exactly. Don't skip steps. Type it yourself instead of copy-pasting.
````

### V06 · T2 Text+Scene · 22 words

````md
5. **Learn in public.** Post your progress, even small things. My LinkedIn posts helped me get two job offers in one day.
````

### V07 · T2 Text+Scene · 22 words

````md
6. **Use certifications for the foundation, not the badge.** A pile of projects can still leave gaps. A good certification fills them.
````

### V08 · T2 Text+Scene · 19 words

````md
7. **Find a mentor as early as you can.** It doesn't matter how experienced you are. Everything keeps changing.
````

### V09 · T2 Text+Scene · 21 words

````md
8. **Fix your communication.** I hid behind emails for years because of my English. Don't wait 20 years like I did.
````

### V10 · T2 Text+Scene · 19 words

````md
9. **Respect the foundation.** Beautiful charts on bad data will mislead people. Data quality and metadata are not extras.
````

### V11 · T2 Text+Scene · 34 words

````md
10. **It's never too late.** I was over 40, my company had just closed, and I was back to being a student. A year later, I had a new career in a new industry.
````
