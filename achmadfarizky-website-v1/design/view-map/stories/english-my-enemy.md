````md
# From emergency to fluency: retraining my voice

As a data professional, I know the dream: a solid data model, clear business logic and beautiful visuals. But for most of my career, one important piece was missing: **clear communication.**
````

### V02 · T2 Text+Scene · 69 words

````md
## English was my enemy

When I first moved to Saudi Arabia in 2010, my English speaking was close to zero. My Arabic helped me survive. My English just "grew by nature," without any real study. To be honest, it was a disaster.

In data analytics, we want our audience to say *"Aha!"* when they see a chart. But when I spoke, I often heard *"What did he say?"*
````

### V03 · T2 Text+Scene · 20 words

````md
English was my enemy for 20 years of my career, mostly because I was too lazy to start learning it.
````

### V04 · T2 Text+Scene · 54 words

````md
## What bad English really cost me

- **I hid behind text.** I always preferred typing an email over making a phone call. That delayed my work, because I had to wait for replies. I was nervous every time my desk phone rang. Not because of the person calling, but because of my speaking.
````

### V05 · T2 Text+Scene · 66 words

````md
- **I skipped live sessions.** I avoided live classes with global experts and only watched the recordings, because I was afraid someone would ask me a question. I lost many chances to learn directly from the best.
- **I avoided the spotlight.** I even turned down interviews with people who saw me as an expert. I had the knowledge, but not the voice to share it.
````

### V06 · T2 Text+Scene · 67 words

````md
## Retraining my voice

In late 2025, I decided to fix it. I took the American English Pronunciation course by A.J. Hoge. In my March 2026 post, I shared a video of my English "today" next to how I spoke "a few months ago." The funny part: my facial muscles had already changed from practice, so it was hard to sound as bad as I really was!
````

### V07 · T2 Text+Scene · 52 words

````md
My view on accents: having an accent is normal and should be respected. But clear pronunciation is a must. **An accent is about identity. Pronunciation is about being understood.** If the accent is too strong, the message gets lost. I want my message to be the hero, not my struggle with sounds.
````

### V08 · T2 Text+Scene · 57 words

````md
## My first English test, ever

In June 2026, I took my first English proficiency test: the **Duolingo English Test**. (It's a real proficiency test accepted by thousands of institutions, not the Duolingo app.) You can take it from home, and it costs about $70, much cheaper than IELTS or TOEFL. But the test itself isn't easy.
````

### V09 · T2 Text+Scene · 62 words

````md
I prepared with Teacher Luke Jones's DET Ready program. My first practice score was 105. I practiced every day until I reached 120 in practice. And on the real test, I scored **120**, about the same as IELTS band 6.5.

I know that's "average," not fluent yet. Many of you are already at this level or above. But I was so happy.
````

````md
- Don't wait 20 years like I did. Start now, even with ten minutes a day.
- Communication is part of data work. The best analysis is useless if nobody understands what you're saying.
````
