7. Johns Hopkins Aramco Healthcare: learning a new language (2025–now)

Context
In October 2025, I joined Johns Hopkins Aramco Healthcare (JHAH) in Dhahran as a Quality & Data Analyst, working as a data specialist in healthcare. It was my third industry, and the first one where I didn't know the business at all.

or press Enter

What I did

Before day one, I studied healthcare analytics and passed the CHDA exam (AHIMA), which shocked me with how deep the healthcare questions were. Then Elijah Joshua Pryor, through his posts, introduced me to DAMA and the DMBOK2. After that, I started studying data management seriously, because it brings all my years of experience together into one framework.

or press Enter

In my first year:

  • I found that about 60% of my job is talking to people, not working with data.
  • My first task was one page of a Power BI dashboard. A few weeks later, I was unexpectedly asked to open it in a meeting with shareholders and answer their questions. They liked it.
  • My director gave me the chance to lead a new data project, where I practiced data modeling for real.
  • I took Epic training (Cogito, Clarity, Caboodle and Access data models) to understand our healthcare data and work better with the IT reporting teams.
  • I helped with the data side of JHAH's ICHOM accreditation for two services, one of them a first in Saudi Arabia.
  • I passed all my CDMP exams and became a CDMP Master in August 2026.
or press Enter

My work moved from front-end dashboards to the back end: data modeling, data quality and metadata. I miss building Power BI experiments sometimes, but a strong back end makes the front end much easier.

or press Enter

Now I'm studying again: a Master of Applied Science in Patient Safety & Healthcare Quality at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, and two AI and Agentic AI in Healthcare programs at the Johns Hopkins Whiting School of Engineering.

or press Enter

What I learned

  • Thread 2, in its newest form: learn their language. To build something useful for doctors, nurses and pharmacists, I first need to understand how they work. So I'm learning healthcare from the inside.
or press Enter
  • Find mentors early. My colleagues are senior people with clinical backgrounds, and my manager is strong in data management. I'm surrounded by great teachers.
  • Analytics shows you the answer. Data management tells you whether you can trust it.
or press Enter

A moment I'm proud of
That shareholder meeting, when the dashboard answered their questions better than I could.

or press Enter

Tools I picked up

DAMA-DMBOK (data modeling, DW/BI, data quality) · Epic Clarity/Caboodle/Cogito · Healthcare statistics · Power BI · PowerPoint (yes, still)
