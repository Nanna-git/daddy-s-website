Why must a data analyst learn the business?

I'm sure many experts have already answered this question. So I'll just share one of my own stories.

My first task

When I joined Johns Hopkins Aramco Healthcare as a data analyst, my first task was to build one page of a larger Power BI dashboard: the KPIs for oncology medication.

To build it well, I had to understand what the clinical teams were measuring, and why. Not just the numbers, but the questions behind them.

The unexpected meeting

A few weeks ago, my manager invited me to a virtual meeting with shareholders in the US, because the oncology medication KPIs were one of the topics.

Then, unexpectedly, I was asked to open the dashboard and answer some of their questions. Live.

Alhamdulillah, the dashboard answered their questions, and even gave them more useful insights than they asked for. At the end, one shareholder said he liked the dashboard, both the content and the way the information was presented. Others agreed and thanked us too.

(You know what "shareholder" means, right? Think about who they are in an organization.)

Thank you to my leaders, my managers and my director, for always giving me room to grow and to learn more about healthcare.

Behind every successful professional…

There's a saying: behind a successful man is his wife. I'd add: behind a successful professional are one or more great mentors.

The people behind this dashboard:

  • Power BI Park and Injae Park, where I learned Power BI development tools like TMDL, JSON themes, UDFs and macros, and my favorite: the analytics mindset.
  • The Data Vis Forge and Gustaw Dudek, where I learned enterprise-style reporting, UI/UX in Power BI, and how to orchestrate field parameters.
  • The data storytelling experts who share their knowledge in books, portfolios and posts: Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic (Storytelling with Data), Janine Kurnoff and Lee Lazarus (Everyday Business Storytelling, the famous yellow book), Nick Desbarats (More Practical Charts), Armand van Amersfoort (my first inspiration for web-style reports), Iwa Sanjaya, Nicholas Lea-Trengrouse and Naresh Suglani (professional BI developers), and Julien Charles-Donatien (the "Power BI therapist" and UI/UX expert).

You're all amazing. Keep sharing.

What I learned

  • Stay close to the business. A dashboard can only answer questions you understand.
  • Learning the tools got me the job. Learning the business made the dashboard useful in a room full of people I'd never met.
