The CHDA exam shocked me

A new world

Before joining a healthcare organization, I had never worked in healthcare. I was surprised to learn that healthcare data analytics is its own specialized field, with its own professional certificate: the Certified Health Data Analyst (CHDA) from AHIMA.

So I started reading A Practical Approach to Analyzing Healthcare Data by Susan White. What surprised me most was the strong focus on inferential statistics: hypothesis testing, t-tests, z-tests, ANOVA, chi-squared. Research and surveys, based on samples, are at the heart of healthcare analysis.

That's very different from my years in telecom network operations, where analysis was mostly descriptive and we used the full dataset for every calculation.

I also found great videos from Joshua Matlock, a senior healthcare data analyst in the US, on his "Data Wizardry" YouTube channel, and an introduction to healthcare analysis from Alex Freberg.

Two weeks, then the exam

I studied for two weeks, then took the exam.

Question one. Question two. Question three… up to question ten. And I was shocked. The questions were much deeper and more complex than what I had studied. So many medical terms and real cases, mixed into the analytics questions.

Here's an example of the difference:

Practice version: Which graph is best for comparing lengths of stay across four hospitals? (Line chart, histogram, bar chart, scatter plot.) Easy: bar chart.

Real exam version: The laboratory manager wants to evaluate the [something] of the [system] using the [standard] for accreditation purposes. As a data analyst, you must prepare the report following the rules of [organization]. Which visualization should you use? Hmm…

This exam really shows how strong the link is between the business domain and data analytics. If you don't understand the business, you can't deliver good analysis.

The sad face

According to AHIMA, only 49% of first-time test-takers passed in 2024. It's known as a hard exam. Failing means paying the $329 fee again and waiting 90 days before you can retake it, or giving up.

I finished the exam with a sad face.

I sat outside the exam room, waiting for the officer to bring my result paper. When he came, he said: "Congratulations! You passed the exam."

Really?! Alhamdulillah.

The CHDA exam, from my experience

  • About 15%: healthcare foundations. The healthcare delivery system, datasets, organizations, accreditation agencies, regulations, standards and the revenue cycle. Lots of acronyms: SNOMED-CT, CPT, ICD-10-CM, CMS, DICOM, DRG, HCPCS, HIPAA, LOINC and more.
  • About 15%: data analytics foundations. SQL and data modeling. Relatively easy for a junior data analyst.
  • About 70%: analytics, mostly statistics in healthcare cases. Hypothesis testing, sampling methods, z-tests, t-tests. You need both memorization and the healthcare foundation, because many questions that look easy from a data point of view become hard because of the domain.

What I learned

Stay close to the business. This exam proved it in the hardest way. In a new industry, the data skills you bring are only half of what you need. The other half is learning the business.
