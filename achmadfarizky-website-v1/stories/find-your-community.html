Find your community

I'm seriously amazed by the power of community-based learning.

People in good communities help each other. They solve each other's problems, and they share real-world wins (and lessons learned) from their own projects. And the leaders keep the content fresh, always adding the latest things.

2010: my first community

My first experience with community learning was in 2010, with Pentaho-Indonesia, led by Feris Thia.

At that time, I was working on my first data warehouse project at Telkomsel, with Pentaho, my first data warehouse and BI tool. Feris wasn't only the community leader. He was also my mentor on that project. Having a place to ask questions, and someone who actually knew the answers, made a huge difference.

2025: communities again

Fifteen years later, communities changed my career again.

  • Power BI Park, led by Injae Park. I joined in January 2025. By April, I had been at the top of the 30-day leaderboard for three months.
  • Fabric Dojo, led by Will Needham. A few days after joining, I earned my green belt and was in the top 3 of the leaderboard.
  • Later, The Data Vis Forge, led by Gustaw Dudek, for advanced Power BI front-end workouts.

Thank you to everyone in these communities for appreciating and supporting my learning journey.

Why it works

  • You're not alone. When you're stuck, someone has usually been stuck in the same place.
  • You learn from real projects. Not only tutorials, but people's actual problems at work.
  • You stay current. Good community leaders keep adding new content as the tools change.
  • It keeps you going. A leaderboard, a belt, a "great job" from someone you respect. Small things, but they push you forward.

If you're looking to grow

Find your community. Show up. Follow the instructions. Help others when you can.

It worked for me in 2010, and it worked again in 2025.
