The Hajj dream team

Every year, millions of pilgrims come to Makkah for Hajj. For a few days, a huge number of people are in a small area at the same time, and many of them are using their phones.

For a mobile network, that's one of the biggest tests of the year. And for the operations team, every minute counts. They need to see what is happening on the network now, not tomorrow morning in a report.

Data leader for Hajj

At Ericsson, I supported Mobily for nine years. From 2013 to 2020, I was appointed as the data leader for Hajj affairs.

My job was to make sure the team could see everything they needed, live:

  • I integrated the data end to end, from many network sources into one place.
  • I built dashboards and a mini-GIS application for live monitoring on a map.
  • I made advanced reports for the team and management.
  • I automated many processes that used to be done by hand, which saved a lot of effort and improved the accuracy.

These tools weren't made for one event. They were used for many Hajj seasons in a row.

More than a team

But what I remember most is not the tools. It's the people.

Working with that team was more than sharing the workload. It was a family. We supported each other to grow, with no hidden motives. We taught each other and learned from each other. We covered for each other and solved each other's problems.

When Mobily shared the success of the 2023 Hajj season, I wrote a post to thank the team: Naif Al Malki, Arham Awal, Abdullah Alwarthan, Maher Hindeleh, Mohammad Rabee Alzoubi and Benedicto Sarmiento.

Naif, who was a General Manager at Mobily and my data analytics mentor for many years, replied:

"Indeed, your fingerprint is still till today, you are one of the best talented telecom engineer in advanced data analysis field that I worked with him, wish you all the best."

I still read that comment sometimes.

What I learned

  • Stay close to the business. The best tools I built for Hajj came from listening to the operations team: what they needed to see, how fast, and in what form.
  • Build with what you have. We didn't buy a big new system. We connected the data we already had and built the views the team needed.
  • People make the work. Good tools help, but a team that supports each other is what gets you through the hardest week of the year.

Serving the guests of Allah during Hajj, even from behind a screen, is an honor I'm grateful for.
