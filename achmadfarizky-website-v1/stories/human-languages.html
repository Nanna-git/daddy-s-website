Human languages beat programming languages

a: آپ کہاں رہتی ہیں؟
b: میں الرياض میں رہتی ہوں

(That's Urdu, written in the Nastaliq script. "Where do you live?" "I live in Riyadh.")

The languages I code in

Many data analysts, me included, use reporting and BI tools that are ready to use. But the experts I know in those tools are also programmers. At least, they know some programming languages that help their work. I believe this is one of the keys that makes them experts.

I use a few programming languages too: SQL, Excel VBA, M for Power Query, DAX, Python, and React for web front-ends. Knowing even a piece of each of them really helps my work.

The languages I live in

But there's another kind of language I want to talk about: human language.

As a foreigner living in an Arab country, surrounded by people from many countries, I can say this: learning human languages is as important as learning programming languages for a data analyst.

  • Mandarin. In junior high school, many of my classmates were Chinese, and I was very close to some of them. They invited me to their homes. We played ping-pong, had lunch with their families, and visited their parents' shops. I spoke a little Mandarin then.
  • Arabic. I studied formal Arabic and the Saudi Hijazi dialect, because I've lived in Saudi Arabia for many years and I need the local language every day. (Jordanian and Egyptian Arabic are common here too.)
  • Urdu. A few years ago, I decided to learn Urdu seriously, because there are so many people from Pakistan around me: at home, in the office, in the markets, on the street. Urdu is very close to Hindi, so it would help me talk with my Indian friends too. Unfortunately, I only studied it for two weeks before other priorities took over. I hope I can continue one day.

Why it matters

Speaking someone's language, even a little, shows respect to the person you're talking to. And it makes daily life, and work, so much easier.

If I could only choose one, mastering programming languages or human languages? I'd choose human languages. If I know the programmers' mother tongues well, I can work with any of them.

(Funny enough, the language I avoided the longest was English. That's another story: From emergency to fluency.)
