I really miss building front-end experiments 📊

In 2025, I was deeply active in communities like Power BI Park and The Data Vis Forge, building all kinds of visualization experiments.

In 2026, my role moved strongly to the back end: data modeling, data quality and metadata architecture.

Even so, I still follow Power BI updates, and great mentors like Injae Park, Gustaw Dudek, Armand van Amersfoort, Michel van Schaik and others.

The Gantt chart moment

Recently, everyone has been talking about Power BI's new Gantt chart visual. It reminded me of a Gantt-style timeline I built by hand back in August 2025, from my Trello data.

Building that timeline manually was so satisfying. And I'm happy to see how much easier the October 2026 Power BI release makes it now!

Just so you know: back end makes front end better

Working on the back end actually makes your front-end work much more precise and effective.

A real example: Johns Hopkins Aramco Healthcare recently achieved ICHOM accreditation for two services, and one of them was a first in Saudi Arabia.

Behind the scenes, I helped bring the data together: from modeling, to quality, to metadata, to visualization, with great support from the IT application and reporting teams, data analytics, and the clinical support teams.

Because the back end was strong and well-structured, building the final Power BI dashboard took me only a short time. Of course, what I learned from the communities and BI experts helped a lot too.

What I learned

  • Keep practicing, and stay close to the business!
  • A beautiful dashboard on a weak foundation is fragile. A simple dashboard on a strong foundation is fast, and it lasts.
