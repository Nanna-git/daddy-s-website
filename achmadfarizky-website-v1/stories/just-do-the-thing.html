Just do the thing!

In 2025, I focused on studying Power BI. That year of learning helped me get a good job in a good company, with a great team, and move into a completely new industry: from telecom engineering to healthcare.

People sometimes ask me what my secret was. Honestly, it's simple:

I followed the instructions exactly.

Two communities, one rule

I joined two great communities: Power BI Park, with Prof. Injae Park, and The Data Vis Forge, with Master Gustaw Dudek.

I received a lot of appreciation from them, in the communities, on LinkedIn and in private messages. But in my mind, I was the one appreciating them: by respecting their guidelines and following every instruction. Then I showed the world how good their study materials were.

My process

▪️ Follow the instructions.
If the mentor said "create the dataset," "post on LinkedIn," "build your portfolio," "do this" or "do that," I did exactly that. I didn't skip any steps. Even posting on LinkedIn twice a week was not my own idea. It was the community's instruction.

▪️ No shortcuts.
In Injae's class, he gave us ready-made datasets. I typed everything myself anyway. I wrote the C# macro scripts in Tabular Editor by hand, just to mirror what he did in the video. (Typing code is like lifting dumbbells: more reps, more pain, but no pain, no gain!)

▪️ Rebuild everything.
In Gustaw's exercises, I didn't just copy-paste from his finished PBIX files. I rebuilt the visuals and the code by myself, matching his colors, styles and formulas. By doing this, I found "hidden treasures" in his work that I would have missed if I had only looked at the final result.

After I finished each lesson, I sometimes added my own style, just to show my identity. But that step isn't necessary. Rebuilding the mentor's work exactly is already a great way to learn.

An old habit

Looking back, this isn't new for me. In 2021, when I was learning machine learning with Python, I finished my first exercise in an hour and a half "without any copy-paste, just typing by myself." The tools changed. The habit didn't.

If you want to learn something new

  • Don't overthink.
  • Don't skip steps.
  • Type it yourself.

Just do the thing!
