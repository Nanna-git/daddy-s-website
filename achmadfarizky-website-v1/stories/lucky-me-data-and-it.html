Data in IT is worse than a headache? Lucky me. 🧠

I've seen many posts from experts saying that if IT controls all the data, the company will fail.

Well, lucky me.

Years on the business side

I worked in telecom for many years as a data specialist on the business side. And I never had a problem with IT blocking me or limiting my access to data.

In fact, the IT teams were very supportive. I could build whatever I needed: custom reports, data models, email tools and automation. I even used the BI web server to host small extra apps.

Why did I build so much? Because the vendor's built-in reports didn't always meet our business needs. Custom was the solution.

IT simply told us: "Go ahead! Do what the business needs. We'll monitor the servers and apps, and tell you if they get too busy." And we said: "Thank you!"

When I was asked to support other projects in the GCC and Africa, the project managers gave me full control of the BI tools, so I could quickly copy what I had already built.

The results? A project manager in Bahrain told me, "Your reports are great!" A customer director in Benin said, "I like that dashboard!" (And remember, he was from the customer's company, the one paying for our services.)

And now, in healthcare?

So how is it in my current job? Lucky me, again!

I recently finished intensive training to better understand how complex healthcare data is, to appreciate the work of the IT applications and reporting teams, and to make the collaboration between IT and analytics stronger. I'm now certified in:

  • Epic Cogito Fundamentals (the reporting tools inside the Epic system),
  • Epic Caboodle Data Model (the database for analytics),
  • Epic Clarity Data Model (the database for daily records),
  • Epic Access Data Model (how patient access connects to the data).

We're also invited to regular meetings with the IT reporting teams, managers and directors, to share business needs, problems and solutions.

Thank you to the people on both the business and IT sides who sponsor this collaboration, and of course to our key players: the IT reporting and application support developers.

No CDO? No problem

We don't have an official data department led by a Chief Data Officer. But running a good data system is possible, and even necessary, without one.

And if a CDO ever joins us? Lucky them, to find people who are already using good data practices.

What I learned

  • Stay close to the business, and to IT. Good data work needs both sides talking.
  • Respect goes both ways. When you understand IT's world, they trust you with more.
