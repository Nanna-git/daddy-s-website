My Power BI Park lab

At the end of 2024, I decided to learn Power BI properly. I already had years of BI experience with SAP BusinessObjects, and I knew the basics of Power BI. But I wanted to learn it the modern way.

I chose Power BI Park, Injae Park's community, because it's project-based. Instead of learning feature by feature, you build real reports, one after another. I joined on January 17, 2025.

For me, it felt like a lab for experiments.

The workout log

Here's what I built in my first three months:

  1. IT Service Tickets dashboard. My first project: SLA performance, resolution times, and some tricky conditional formatting for critical issues. I generated the dataset with a Python script (with help from Gemini).
  2. Electronic Sales dashboard. More advanced bar chart techniques, DAX in the query view, and an image for visual appeal. Later I made a second version, with a bar chart inspired by Megan Menth's style. (One challenge was using different colors for each region. I skipped it. I just liked it better with one color.)
  3. A website inside Power BI. Based on the design of a job-board website. Who knew you could build a website in Power BI?!
  4. Bar charts, upgraded. A simple bar chart, presented in many advanced ways, inspired by Andy Kriebel, Kurt Buhler, Nicholas Lea-Trengrouse and Rosa Mariana de León-Escribano. I added my own progress bars using background images, and one with DAX inspired by Jasmin Simader and Raja Ayub.
  5. HR Overview. My fourth project. I added a monthly salary for each employee, color-coded changes vs. last year (green when terminations go down, red when they go up), and turned the KPI cards into buttons with a hover effect.
  6. The new card visual + SVG built with DAX. CONCATENATEX, FILTER, SUMMARIZE and ADDCOLUMNS, all working together to generate SVG code. My brain got a real workout.
  7. SVG macros. A collection of reusable graphics in Tabular Editor, from Injae's classroom, Kurt Buhler's "Data Goblin," and my own.
  8. C# macros, typed by hand. I'm building a macro that creates documentation for any report in a minute, inspired by Martyn Booth. And I typed every script manually. Typing code is like lifting dumbbells: no pain, no gain!
  9. My own JSON theme, so I don't have to touch the Format pane every time. (Bye bye, Format pane! Hmm… I think I still need it.)
  10. Learning from the champions. Injae showed how he won a challenge in 2022 with a three-layer approach: explore everything, then curate the key insights, then polish the design. I realized I often jump straight to the polish, which is totally wrong.

The results

After only three weeks, I earned the community's Power BI Visualization certification. I was at the top of the 30-day leaderboard from January to March, which in this community means consistent work over time, not one lucky week. In March 2025, Injae asked me to become a moderator.

And the feedback from Injae himself made my day more than once:

"You've taken it even further than anyone else. Great job." (on the HR Overview)

"This doesn't even look like Power BI anymore. Great job Achmad Farizky." (on the web-style report)

What I learned

  • Just do the thing. Injae asked us to post our progress on LinkedIn twice a week. I did. That was the start of everything that happened later in 2025.
  • Power BI Park taught me much more than visuals: C# for Tabular Editor, TMDL, JSON themes, personal branding, and my favorite, the analytics mindset.
  • Thank you, Prof. Injae. 감사합니다.

(I also offered help to new members in Power BI Park, with Power Query, data modeling, DAX and visualization. And as I told them: I only analyze the relationships among your data tables. I don't help with your real-life relationships.)
