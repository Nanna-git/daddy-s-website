My data storytelling bookshelf 📚

The Storytelling with Data family

In November 2025, a box arrived at my place in Saudi Arabia: four books by Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic.

  • Storytelling with Data
  • Storytelling with Data: Let's Practice!
  • Storytelling with Data: Before & After
  • Storytelling with You

One line stays with me: once the numbers are accurate, presenting them properly is the crucial step you can't skip.

Welcome to the club, Yellow! ⭐

A few months later, after working hours, I had a short talk with my director about how we could improve the data storytelling, both the narrative and the visuals, in one of our top management reports.

About 30 minutes later, I opened LinkedIn. At the top of my feed was a post from Morgan Depenbusch:

"If I were a director of an analytics team, I'd immediately gift every person these two books: (1) Everyday Business Storytelling (2) Storytelling with Data."

What a coincidence! We had just talked about exactly this.

I forwarded the post to my director. Soon after, the yellow book, Everyday Business Storytelling by Janine Kurnoff and Lee Lazarus, arrived in our office bookcase. Storytelling with Data had already been there for four months.

I also have two great books on business charts in digital form, because the printed versions can't be shipped to Saudi Arabia: Practical Charts and More Practical Charts by Nick Desbarats.

From the books to the reports

So far, I've finished Storytelling with Data and I'm almost done with Practical Charts. I've already used many of their ideas in the reports I manage, and the results have been great.

People who know me know how much I care about visualization, both beauty and effectiveness, in every project. I always treated it as a must, even when others saw it as a bonus.

Now, thanks to these authors and everyone who keeps promoting good data storytelling, I feel more confident to push this topic forward.

It's not a bonus. It's mandatory. 💪

What I learned

  • Good books give you words for things you already felt. That makes it easier to convince others.
  • Share what you read with your team. Sometimes one forwarded post is enough to get a book on the office shelf.
