2. Telkomsel: the desk guy who discovered data (2005–2009)

Context
After graduating in 2005, I joined Telkomsel, Indonesia's biggest mobile operator, as a radio network engineer in operations and network performance, first in Medan and later in Jakarta.

or press Enter

Honestly, I was a bit jealous in my first years. My colleagues travelled for technical training and went to the field with drive test tools. I stayed at my desk with spreadsheets. One night I begged to join them, and they sent me to a rural site at 2 AM just to make a test call. I was so happy, like a kid in an amusement park.

or press Enter

What I did
At my desk, I started doing more than delivering numbers. I learned UNIX scripting by myself and used it to build small automations for our daily work. I used my old web skills to build a web dashboard for network performance. Then I combined Google Earth with UNIX scripts to build a live GIS view of our 2G radio network, so management could see how the network was running from their own workstations.

or press Enter

In my last year, my manager, Akhmad Madces, brought me into a big data warehouse project. We collected network performance data from all our equipment vendors (Ericsson, Siemens, Nokia, Huawei and more) into one place, and built the dashboards and reports on top of it with Pentaho. I had two great mentors on that project: Reza Lesmana, a solution architect at Nokia, and Feris Thia, CEO of Pentaho Indonesia. It was my first time learning what a data warehouse is, and I still use those concepts today.

or press Enter

What I learned

Thread 1 again. Very few people around me knew even a little programming. That small skill made me useful in a way I didn't expect.

or press Enter

Thread 2 again. My team leaders, Petrus Sila and Sorpa EA Togatorop, kept me at my desk with Excel. At the time I didn't understand why. Later I realized they saw where my work created real value before I did. I wasn't just their "calculator guy." I was helping guide their decisions.

or press Enter

Thread 3, for the first time. Google Earth and a few scripts became a live monitoring tool. No big budget, just tools we already had.

or press Enter

A moment I'm proud of
In 2009, after the earthquake in West Sumatra, my GIS live monitoring tool was used during the network recovery. I received official appreciation from the company's directors and VPs. I was also named best employee of the service quality assurance team in 2007.

Tools I picked up

  • Excel
  • Ericsson OSS
  • Google Earth (KML)
  • HTML/JavaScript
  • Pentaho (Kettle)
  • Data warehousing
