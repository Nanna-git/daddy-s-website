1. University: an engineer who loved design (2000–2005)

Context
In 2000, I started studying Telecommunication Engineering at Telkom University in Bandung, Indonesia. (Back then, it was called Sekolah Tinggi Teknologi Telkom, or STT Telkom.)

or press Enter

My classes were real engineering stuff: switching techniques, traffic engineering, transmission lines, antenna and propagation, digital signal processing, and optical and satellite communication systems. There was a lot of calculus, physics and lab work, and I enjoyed it. Programming was only a small part: one introduction to computers and one programming lab.

or press Enter

What I did
But outside the classroom, I spent a lot of my time on something else: design. I taught myself Adobe Photoshop and CorelDraw, then web design with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and animation with Macromedia Flash. Nobody asked me to. I just liked making things look good.

or press Enter

I had a friend, Jockie Heruseon, who was much better than me at networking. He always knew where the opportunities were. So we teamed up: he found them, and I did the design work. We joined web design competitions, and we got projects to design the university's yearly academic book and the university calendar. Together, we learned how the real world works, long before we graduated.

or press Enter

I also had a best "art friend" on campus, Fakhrudin. He was a famous animator and designer among the students, and we pushed each other to make better work. Years later, when I made animated SVG visuals in Power BI, it took me straight back to those Flash days.

or press Enter

What I learned

Thread 1

Design wasn't part of my engineering degree, but I learned it anyway because I loved it. I didn't know then that it would become a big part of how I present data for the next 20 years.

or press Enter

What I learned

Thread 2

My friend saw the opportunities; I knew how to deliver. Neither of us could have done those projects alone. I've been working this way ever since.

or press Enter

A moment I'm proud of
Getting paid, as a student, to design my own university's calendar and its academic book. Those were my first real design clients.
